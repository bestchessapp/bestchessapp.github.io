Boden's Mate

Two Bishops Cross

Boden's mate is named after Samuel Boden, who won with it in 1853. Two bishops attack along crossing diagonals, and the king's own pieces block every other square.

It often appears against a king that castled queenside. A queen sacrifice tears open the diagonal, and the second bishop gives mate.

The idea

  • Two bishops on diagonals that cross in front of the king.
  • The king's own pieces (a rook, a knight, a pawn) take away its escape squares.
  • A queen sacrifice on c6 or c3 often opens the deciding diagonal.

White to move

The classic combination: a queen sacrifice opens the diagonal.

  1. 1. Qxc6+Check! The queen is lost, but the b-pawn must take it.
  2. 1… bxc6Forced: no other move stops the check.
  3. 2. Ba6#Boden's mate.

After the moves

The b-pawn moved away and the diagonal opened.

How to spot it in your games

  • Look for a king castled queenside with an open or opening a6–f1 diagonal.
  • If one bishop already controls the escape squares, find a check for the other.
  • A sacrifice that forces a pawn to capture can open the diagonal for good.
