Légal's Mate

Give Up the Queen, Mate

Légal's mate is named after Sire de Légal, the strongest player in Paris in the 1700s and the teacher of Philidor. White seems to blunder the queen, but if Black takes it, three minor pieces deliver checkmate.

It works because Black pins White's knight to the queen too early and then plays a slow move. The pin is not real: the knight jumps, and taking the queen walks into mate.

The idea

  • A pin on a knight is only useful if moving the knight really loses something.
  • Bishop on c4, knights on e5 and d5 combine against the king on e7.
  • Greed punished: taking the queen is the mistake.

White to move

You are White. Play the trap.

  1. 1. e4
  2. 1… e5
  3. 2. Nf3
  4. 2… d6
  5. 3. Bc4The bishop aims at f7.
  6. 3… Bg4The bishop pins your knight to the queen.
  7. 4. Nc3Develop. Black now plays a slow move.
  8. 4… g6Too slow: now the trap works.
  9. 5. Nxe5The knight jumps, leaving your queen to be taken!
  10. 5… Bxd1Black takes the queen: a fatal mistake.
  11. 6. Bxf7+Check! The king has only one square.
  12. 6… Ke7
  13. 7. Nd5#Checkmate with three minor pieces.

After the moves

Légal's mate: a pinned knight is not always really pinned.

How to spot it in your games

  • When a pinned knight moves away, ask why: is there a check or a mate behind it?
  • Before taking a queen, check every check the opponent has next.
  • Develop and castle instead of making slow pawn moves after pinning.
