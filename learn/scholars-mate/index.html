Scholar's Mate

Stop the Four-Move Mate

Scholar's mate is the quick checkmate that catches many beginners: White's queen and bishop both aim at f7, the weakest square next to Black's king, and the queen captures there with mate on the fourth move.

It is easy to stop once you know it. Defend f7, develop your pieces, and the early queen becomes a target that you can chase with tempo.

The idea

  • f7 (and f2 for White) is guarded only by the king at the start.
  • Queen plus bishop attacking the same square is a strong battery.
  • Bringing the queen out early is risky if the attack is defended.

White to move

Scholar's mate: the queen and the bishop both attack f7, the weakest square next to Black's king. If Black does not see it, the queen takes on f7 with checkmate.

Key move: 1.e4 e5 2.Bc4 Nc6 3.Qh5 Nf6 4.Qxf7#

Black to move

Now you are Black. Defend f7 and make White's queen look silly.

  1. 1… e5Take your share of the centre.
  2. 2. Bc4
  3. 2… Nc6Develop and protect e5.
  4. 3. Qh5The queen attacks e5 and, with the bishop, f7.
  5. 3… g6Block the queen: f7 is safe now, and e5 stays protected.
  6. 4. Qf3Again two attackers on f7.
  7. 4… Nf6The knight blocks the queen and develops. f7 is safe.

After the moves

Defended. White's queen came out early and wasted time; you are developing with gain of time.

How to spot it in your games

  • If an enemy queen and bishop both aim at f7, block or defend before anything else.
  • Moves like g6 (after Qh5) or Nf6 (after Qf3 is answered) keep f7 safe.
  • After you defend, develop with tempo by attacking the queen.
