Arabian Mate

Rook and Knight in the Corner

The Arabian mate is more than a thousand years old: it appears in problems from the time of shatranj, the ancestor of modern chess. A rook and a knight work together against a king in the corner.

The rook gives check right next to the king, and the knight does two jobs at once: it protects the rook and takes away the king's last escape square.

The idea

  • The king is in the corner (h8, a8, h1 or a1).
  • The rook checks on the square next to the king, along the edge.
  • The knight guards the rook and covers the last free square.

How to spot it in your games

  • A knight two squares from the corner, diagonally (like f6 against a king on h8), is the key piece.
  • Ask: can my rook check on the seventh (or second) rank right next to the king?
  • If the rook is protected, the king cannot take it, and the knight covers the rest.
