Chess Rules

How Chess Pieces Move

King, queen, rook, bishop, knight and pawn

The king

The king moves one square in any direction. It can never move onto a square where it would be attacked. The king is the piece the whole game is about: if it is attacked and cannot escape, the game is lost (checkmate).

The queen

The queen moves any number of squares in a straight line: along ranks, files and diagonals. It is the strongest piece.

The rook

The rook moves any number of squares along a rank or a file, but not diagonally. Rooks are strongest on open files and on the seventh rank.

The bishop

The bishop moves any number of squares diagonally. It always stays on squares of the same colour, so each side has a light-squared and a dark-squared bishop.

The knight

The knight moves in an 'L': two squares in one direction and then one square to the side. It is the only piece that can jump over other pieces, and it always lands on a square of the other colour.

The pawn

A pawn moves straight forward one square. From its starting square it may move two squares instead. Pawns never move backwards.

Pawns capture differently from how they move: one square diagonally forward. Special pawn rules: en passant and promotion (see the guides below).

Capturing and check

Every piece except the pawn captures the same way it moves: it lands on the enemy piece's square and removes it. You can never capture your own pieces, and the king is never captured.

When a king is attacked, it is in check. The player must get out of check at once: move the king, block the attack, or capture the attacking piece.
