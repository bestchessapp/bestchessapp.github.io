Key Squares in Pawn Endgames

Where the King Must Go

Every pawn has key squares. For a pawn that has not passed the middle of the board, they are the three squares two ranks in front of it. If your king reaches one of them, the pawn will queen, whatever the defending king does.

So in king and pawn endgames the race is not about the pawn: it is about your king. Bring it in front of the pawn, onto a key square, and then walk the pawn home.

The idea

  • For a pawn on d4, the key squares are c6, d6 and e6.
  • King on a key square = the pawn queens.
  • The king leads; the pawn follows.

  3. 2. d6Again.

After the moves

King on a key square first, then the pawn: it always works.

How to spot it in your games

  • Before pushing the pawn, ask: can my king reach a key square?
  • Use the opposition (kings facing each other) to gain the key square.
  • Push the pawn only when the king protects every square in front of it.
