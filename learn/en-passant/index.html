En Passant

The pawn capture that surprises beginners

The rule

En passant ('in passing') is a special pawn capture. When a pawn moves two squares forward from its starting square and lands right next to an enemy pawn, that enemy pawn may capture it as if it had moved only one square.

The capturing pawn moves diagonally to the square the other pawn passed over, and the pawn that moved two squares is removed.

White can now capture en passant: exd6. The pawn on d5 is removed.

The timing

The en passant capture must be made immediately, on the very next move. If you wait one move, the chance is gone for good.

Only pawns can capture en passant, and only a pawn that has just moved two squares can be captured this way.

How it is written

In notation it looks like a normal pawn capture: exd6, sometimes followed by 'e.p.'.
