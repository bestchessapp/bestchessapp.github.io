Smothered Mate

A Lone Knight Wins

In a smothered mate the king is so surrounded by its own pieces that it cannot move at all, and a lone knight delivers checkmate. Because a knight jumps, its check can never be blocked.

The most famous version uses a queen sacrifice: the queen checks next to the king, a rook is forced to take it and fills the last escape square, and the knight finishes the job.

The idea

  • The king is usually in the corner, with its own pawns and a rook beside it.
  • A knight check cannot be blocked: if the king cannot move or capture, it is mate.
  • The queen sacrifice forces an enemy piece to block the king's last square.

White to move

The famous combination: give up the queen so that the rook has to block the king's last square. Then the knight mates.

  1. 1. Qg8+Check! The king cannot take: the knight on h6 guards g8.
  2. 1… Rxg8Forced: the rook has to take the queen.
  3. 2. Nf7#Smothered mate.

After the moves

Queen sacrifice, smothered mate. A pattern worth remembering.

How to spot it in your games

  • A king in the corner with pawns in front and a rook next to it is a warning sign.
  • Check whether your knight can reach f7 (or f2) with check.
  • Look for a check on g8 (or g1) that the king cannot answer by capturing.
