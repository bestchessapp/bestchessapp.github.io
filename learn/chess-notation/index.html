Chess Rules

Chess Notation

Read any chess book or game record

Squares

Every square has a name: a letter for the file (a to h, from White's left to right) and a number for the rank (1 to 8, from White's side). The white king starts on e1, the black queen on d8.

The pawn move from e2 to e4 is written simply e4.

Pieces and moves

Each piece has a capital letter: K king, Q queen, R rook, B bishop, N knight. Pawns have no letter. A move is the piece letter plus the square it goes to: Nf3 means a knight goes to f3; e4 means a pawn goes to e4.

Moves are numbered in pairs: 1. e4 e5 2. Nf3 Nc6 means White played e4 and Black e5, then White Nf3 and Black Nc6. A Black move on its own is written 2... Nc6.

Symbols

x means a capture: Bxc6 (a bishop takes on c6), exd5 (the e-pawn takes on d5).

+ means check and # means checkmate: Qh5+ and Qxf7#.

O-O is kingside castling and O-O-O is queenside castling. e8=Q is a promotion.

If two identical pieces can reach the same square, add the file or rank they come from: Nbd2, R1e2.

Annotation marks show the quality of a move: ! good, !! brilliant, ? mistake, ?? blunder, ?! dubious.

PGN and FEN

A whole game is saved as PGN (Portable Game Notation): the moves in notation plus details like the players and the result. A single position is written as FEN, one line that lists every piece. You can turn a FEN into a picture with our free chess diagram maker.
