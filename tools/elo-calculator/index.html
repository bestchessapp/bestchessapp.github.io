How the Elo system works

Every player has a rating. Before a game, the difference between the two ratings gives an expected score: the stronger player is expected to score more. After the game, your rating moves by the K-factor times the difference between what you scored and what you were expected to score.

Expected score: E = 1 / (1 + 10(Ropponent − Ryou) / 400). Rating change: K × (S − E), where S is 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw and 0 for a loss.

Beat a stronger player and you gain a lot; lose to a weaker one and you lose a lot. A 200-point favourite is expected to score about 76%. Following the FIDE rule, a rating difference of more than 400 points is counted as 400.

Which K-factor?

FIDE uses 40 for new players until they have played 30 rated games, and for players under 18 rated below 2300; 20 for most players; and 10 once a player has reached 2400. Online sites use their own systems, so your rating there may move differently.
